Bebop

Bebop is a fast, complex, and highly improvisational style of jazz that emerged in the 1940s. It transformed jazz from danceable entertainment into a sophisticated art form. Musicians like Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, and Thelonious Monk challenged musical conventions, creating a sharp, energetic sound that demanded intense skill and deep musical understanding.

Charlie Parker: Born in Kansas City, Parker is one of the most influential saxophonists in history. A pioneer of bebop, his lightning-fast improvisations and innovative phrasing completely reshaped modern jazz.

Dizzy Gillespie: A groundbreaking trumpeter, composer, and bandleader, Gillespie helped create the bebop movement. Known for his incredible speed and trademark bent trumpet, he pushed jazz into a new era of complexity and creativity.
