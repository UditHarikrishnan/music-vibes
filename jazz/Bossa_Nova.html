Bossa Nova

Bossa nova is a Brazilian musical style that blends the rhythmic drive of samba with the harmonic richness of jazz. Known for soft vocals, syncopated guitar rhythms, and gentle percussion, the genre originated in the 1950s and quickly gained international attention.

João Gilberto: Often called the “father of Bossa Nova,” João Gilberto shaped the genre with his unique guitar style and soft, intimate singing. His groundbreaking work in the 1950s helped define the sound of modern Brazilian jazz.

Antônio Carlos Jobim: One of the most influential composers in Bossa Nova, Jobim helped bridge jazz with Brazilian traditions. Known for classics like *The Girl from Ipanema* and *Wave*, his music shaped the global identity of the genre.
