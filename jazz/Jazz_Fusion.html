Jazz Fusion

Jazz fusion is a genre that emerged in the late 1960s, blending the improvisational freedom of jazz with elements of rock, funk, blues, and world music. Unlike jazz-rock, which leans more towards rock with jazz influence, jazz fusion remains rooted in jazz tradition while expanding its rhythmic and instrumental boundaries. Influential figures of the genre include Masayoshi Takanaka, T-Square, Casiopea, Santana, and the legendary Herbie Hancock.

Herbie Hancock: A pioneer of jazz fusion and one of jazz’s greatest innovators. His work with The Headhunters revolutionized modern jazz. His album Head Hunters is considered one of the greatest jazz albums of all time.

Masayoshi Takanaka: Born in Tokyo in 1953, Takanaka became a major figure in Japanese jazz fusion. Known for albums like Seychelles and The Rainbow Goblins, he has influenced generations of guitarists and continues to perform worldwide.
